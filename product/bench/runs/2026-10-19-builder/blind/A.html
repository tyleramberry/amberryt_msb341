The big idea

A five-dollar pizza sat in a warming rack in Michigan from 2004 until January 2022. The easy explanation is cheap cheese. The reading says that is wrong: the price came from a chain of about a dozen activities, each designed to cost less than the same activity at Domino's or Pizza Hut, and one of them (the delivery driver) was simply deleted.

The four takeaways

  1. Firms do not have costs; activities do. Advantage is won or lost activity by activity.
  2. The advantage often sits between the boxes (linkages), which is why chains are hard to copy.
  3. A value chain shows where cost went out, not who kept the savings. Little Caesars' savings were partly captured by the franchisor. Ask both questions.
  4. Profit pools: profit is often concentrated in a very different part of the chain than revenue. Value chain = efficiency tool (your ledger). Profit pool = positioning tool (industry filings).
The closing challenge: draw your chain, split until boxes have different economics, put a cost and an asset on each, then ask: if you could defend only one activity to the last dollar, which is it, and is it the one you are funding?

Porter's chain (1985)

Michael Porter's radical move: stop treating advantage as a property of "well run" firms. A firm is a bundle of discrete activities. Click any box to see what it means and what Little Caesars does there.

Click a box.

Two things routinely missed

1. The boxes are a starting point. Split an activity when its parts have different economics. "Operations" at an airline is not one thing: gate turnaround and engine overhaul have almost nothing in common. If every activity in your chain has the same cost driver, you have not cut finely enough.
2. Advantage may live between the boxes (linkages). A linkage exists when one activity's cost depends on how another is performed. Examples: spend more on inbound inspection, spend less on rework. Hold more inventory, spend less on expediting. Design a snap-fit part, shorten assembly.

Why rivals cannot copy it

A rival can observe any single activity: store layouts, prices, job postings, patents. What they cannot see is which activity is subsidizing which.

The price advantage has narrowed

Hot-N-Ready price: $5 (2004 to Jan 2022), $5.55 (Jan 2022, with 33% more pepperoni), about $6.79 per third-party trackers now. Domino's has repeatedly run a $9.99 "Best Deal Ever". In July 2025 LC launched its first-ever value menu at $9.99, online only. The reading's read: it was following, not setting, and online-only moves the offer away from the walk-in, no-wait, no-decision mechanic the chain was built for.

Satisfaction (ACSI 2026)

LC wins on price perception (Datassential's value leader), loses on satisfaction. "What a low-price, low-volume position looks like from the outside." Yet net US units still grew by 93 in 2025 while the category shrank.

Verdict: A value chain tells you where cost went out. It does not tell you who kept it. Ask both.

Own one link

The opposite of LC's strategy: take one activity and be so far beyond everyone that the industry has to come to you. Tap a card to flip it for the mechanism.

Pattern: in each case the advantage is a mechanism (chokepoint control, a promise, a lease, externalizing a cost center), not just "being good".

Profit pools

U-Haul (late 1990s): earned a 10% operating margin when consumer truck rental earned under 3%. The money was in boxes, tape, insurance, hitches and storage, bought after the customer signed and stopped comparison shopping. U-Haul held truck rates down on purpose: the low-margin activity acquires the customer for the high-margin one.
Definition (Gadiesh and Gilbert, HBR 1998): total profit earned at every point along an industry's value chain. Profit concentration is often very different from revenue concentration.
Value chain = from your general ledger, shows your costs, efficiency instrument. Profit pool = from industry filings, shows everyone's profit, positioning instrument ("am I standing in the right part of the chain?").

The four steps

  1. Define boundaries. This step determines the answer, and no principle exists for drawing the line.
  2. Estimate total pool size (profit, not revenue).
  3. Estimate each activity's share. Hard; margins are rarely disclosed, so use pure-play comparables and triangulation.
  4. Check and reconcile. A quarter of the method is reconciliation: the authors knew the numbers would not tie out.

Drawing convention: x-axis = share of industry revenue, y-axis = operating margin, so block area = profit. Tall and narrow = small and rich. Wide and flat = large and poor.

One-page cheat sheet

Core claim
Advantage lives in activities and in the links between them, not in "firms."
Porter's 9
Primary: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service. Support: firm infrastructure, HR, technology development, procurement. Margin = willingness to pay minus total cost.
5 analysis steps
1 Disaggregate. 2 Cost and asset per box. 3 Cost drivers. 4 Work the linkages. 5 Own / buy / let someone else's brand ride.
10 cost drivers
Scale, learning, capacity utilization, linkages, interrelationships across units, vertical integration, timing of entry, discretionary policy, location, institutional factors.
Little Caesars
Delete activities (drivers, order-taking, counter). Made-ahead + narrow menu + low price reinforce each other. But: AUV about $950K vs $1,385K, fees 11% vs 9.5%, floor $446.5K vs $107K, price up to $5.55 then about $6.79, ACSI 78 (last of 4).
Own one link
ASML (supplier chokepoint, Zeiss stake), TSMC (promise not to compete), YKK (machines leased into customers' factories), Amazon (cost center sold to rivals' customers), Ritz-Carlton ($2,000 discretion).
Profit pool
Total profit at every point in the chain. 4 steps: boundaries, size, shares, reconcile. Map: x = revenue share, y = margin, area = profit.
Examples
U-Haul accessories. Dealership: P&S + F&I = 22.8% of sales, 77.2% of gross profit. Coffee farm 21%. Delta Amex 141% of op. income. Booking 2x Marriott.
Always ask
Where did the cost go out, and who kept it? Am I standing in the right part of the chain?
