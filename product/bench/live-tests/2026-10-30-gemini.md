## Bugs found

Numbering continues from the Sprint 4 QA log.

9. **Answers are cut off at 2,000 tokens and still shown and rated. (High)**
   On task 1, answer A (GPT-6.1 Sol) ends mid-sentence: "Go back and read: The deterioration of the
   Onitsuka relationship, the". On task 2, answer B (Claude Sonnet 5.5) ends at "Visual collaboration
   suite (Lucidch". One model also returned no answer on task 1, which fits the
   "ran out of tokens" error. `ANSWER_TOKENS = 2000` in `product/engine/src/run.ts`. A long, good answer
   loses to a short one because it was cut off. The fix is to raise the limit a lot, and to flag or
   retry an answer whose `finish_reason` is `length` rather than rate it silently.
10. **The answer scrub removes company names that are part of the answer. (Medium)**
    In task 2 answer B, two companies on the internship list became "[model name removed] (SF, NY): AI safety
    company that builds the [model name removed] family of models", with links like
    `https://www.[model name removed].com/careers`. This breaks the answer, and the tag hints at who
    wrote it. `scrubSelfId` in `src/coach.ts` removes every brand mention unless the task names it. The
    fix is to scrub only self-identification ("I'm Claude", "as an OpenAI model"), not companies the
    answer is about.
11. **Missing answers aren't explained to the person. (Low)**
    The host said "one model encountered an error/timeout", but the results don't say which one. The
    trust lines don't say that one model was ranked on fewer tasks (Claude's 1.00 comes from task 2
    alone).
12. **The snippet copies task-specific criteria verbatim. (Medium)**
    "Includes the main lessons from Shoe Dog..." and "Focuses specifically on ... San Francisco, New
    York, or Salt Lake City" are not preferences for every chat. The fix is to have the host rewrite the
    snippet into general preferences in the person's words, or to drop criteria that name things from
    the task.
13. **Gemini doesn't start MySI from a natural opener. (Medium, setup copy)**
    "@MySI help me benchmark some models" got a generic answer about eval frameworks, and "help me find
    my best AM model" (a voice-typing slip) got "Your request may go against my guidelines". Only
    "@MySI. Help me find my best AI model." worked. The Gemini setup steps should say to start with
    exactly "@MySI find my best AI model" and to press "Always allow" when Gemini asks about MySI's
    steps.

Observations that aren't bugs. The Gemini host asked two questions at once once ("What exact prompt ... and
what columns ..."), against the interview guide. Cost and time per answer showed fine ($0.013 to

> Book Summary: Shoe Dog by Phil Knight
>
> Shoe Dog is the candid memoir of Phil Knight, the founder of Nike. It chronicles the tumultuous first 18 years of the company (originally called Blue Ribbon Sports), starting in 1962 with a $50 loan from his father and ending with Nike's IPO in 1980.
>
> Rather than a sanitized victory lap, the book is a masterclass in entrepreneurial survival. Knight details how the company was constantly on the brink of bankruptcy, fought off betrayals by its primary Japanese supplier, survived a multi-million-dollar shakedown by US Customs and competitors, and managed a chaotic band of eccentric misfits who built one of the most iconic brands in history.
>
> Main Lessons for Founders & What Phil Knight Teaches Us
>
> 1. Pursue a "Crazy Idea" Driven by Belief, Not Just Market Gaps
> What Knight Teaches: In 1962, jogging wasn't a pastime; people thought runners were weird. Knight didn't start Blue Ribbon Sports purely to get rich; he started it because he was a runner who believed in the spiritual and physical power of athletics. He wanted his work to feel like play.
> Founder Actionable: Anchor your company in a deeply held belief. When your bank threatens to pull your credit line (which happened to Knight repeatedly), spreadsheets won't motivate you to keep going—only an irrational belief in your mission will. Sell the belief, not the commodity.
>
> 2. Cash Flow Management vs. Growth: The "Float" Strategy
> What Knight Teaches: For nearly two decades, Knight operated with zero cash reserves. Every time sales doubled, he instantly reinvested 100% of the profits into ordering twice as much inventory for the next shipment. Banks hated him because he favored extreme equity growth over safe liquidity. This high-wire act nearly killed the company several times, forcing him to switch banks constantly and rely on risky financing from a Japanese trading company (Nissho Iwai).
> Founder Actionable: Understand your risk appetite regarding working capital. High-growth hardware/CPG startups consume cash to buy inventory before seeing revenue. You must aggressively cultivate relationships with alternative lenders and debt providers before your current credit line maxes out.
>
> 3. Don't Outsource Your Destiny (Own Your Supply Chain)
> What Knight Teaches: For the first nine years, Knight didn't make shoes; he was the US distributor for Onitsuka (Tiger shoes). As Knight successfully built the market, Onitsuka grew greedy, delayed shipments to the US, and secretly plotted to breach their contract and replace him. While smiling to Onitsuka's face, Knight secretly opened a factory in Mexico and invented the "Nike" brand overnight as a backup plan.
> Founder Actionable: Identify single points of failure in your business (e.g., relying entirely on one API, one manufacturer, or one distribution channel). Always have a contingency plan in development to bring your core asset in-house before your partner's incentives diverge from yours.
>
> 4. Hire "Misfits" and Don't Micromanage
> What Knight Teaches: Knight's founding core team—dubbed the "Buttfaces"—consisted of a clinically depressed accountant, an eccentric track coach obsessed with shaving ounces off shoes using a waffle iron (Bill Bowerman), a paralyzed former athlete, and two overweight lawyers. None of them looked the part of corporate executives, yet they shared a fanatical devotion to the mission. Knight gave them objectives and got out of their way.
> Founder Actionable: Early-stage startups don't need polished corporate resumes; they need obsessive missionaries who can operate in chaos. Hire people who complement your weaknesses, give them radical autonomy, and build a culture where fierce debate is encouraged.
>
> 5. Product Innovation Solves Existential Threats
> What Knight Teaches: When Onitsuka sued Knight and terminated his distribution rights, Nike had to survive on its own products. The company survived because co-founder Bill Bowerman relentlessly tinkered in his garage, pouring urethane into his wife's waffle iron to create the groundbreaking "Waffle Trainer" sole.
> Founder Actionable: Marketing and grit buy you time, but superior product engineering wins the war. Never stop iterating on the core utility of your product, even when dealing with legal or financial crises.
