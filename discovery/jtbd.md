# Homeroom: Job to Be Done (baseline v0)

**Date:** 2026-10-03 · **Status:** Hypothesis. No customer data yet. Companion to [`icp.md`](icp.md). Sprint 2 interviews will confirm or kill each line.

## Job statement

> "When I have a rubric-graded assignment due, I want to know my draft hits every criterion **before** I submit, without re-pasting my course into a chatbot or putting myself on the wrong side of my professor's AI policy, **so I can turn it in confident it will score well.**"

## The three layers of the job

| Layer | The job | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional** | Check the draft against the real rubric, criterion by criterion, and know what to fix | "Tell me what's missing before I submit." |
| **Emotional** | Feel confident, not anxious, when I hit submit; stop second-guessing the draft at midnight | "I just want to know it's good enough." |
| **Social** | Use AI without looking like a cheater to my professor or classmates; stay inside the honor code | "I'm getting feedback, not having it written for me." |

The social layer is what separates this job from "make my essay better." Fear of a cheating accusation is a top student worry (48%, Pearson 2025). BYU's default is that if the professor hasn't said anything, AI is not allowed. Homeroom has to be something a student would be comfortable telling their professor they used.

## Pains today

- Generic AI answers that don't know the course.
- Re-pasting context every session.
- Fear of a cheating accusation (48% of students, Pearson 2025).
- Unclear rules. BYU's default is that if the professor hasn't said anything, assume AI is not allowed.

## Current workarounds (what they hire today)

- ChatGPT or Gemini with the rubric pasted in.
- Grammarly's AI Grader, where you upload the rubric yourself and the free tier is capped.
- The Canvas to-do list.

## When they go looking

- The first big paper of the semester.
- A weak grade that comes back with rubric comments.
- Midterms, which are now.
- Finals, weeks 13–15.

## What to listen for in interviews

- [ ] Do students describe the outcome as **confidence** ("know it's good"), or as a **grade** ("get an A")? That decides how the rubric check is worded.
- [ ] Does the social worry come up unprompted? If no one mentions professors or the honor code, the social layer may be weaker than assumed.
- [ ] Which workaround do they actually use, and what made them stop or keep using it?
