## What happened

She connected MySI in Claude, described two things she uses AI for, and approved two tests:

1. **Email writing.** Follow-up to a wedding planner who picked up a tasting box. Her twist: the
   referral program changed, but this planner should get it "what I originally told her."
   A great reply "gets the details, is respectful, and uses an excited tone," and "pays attention
   to follow-up questions that I need information for." It annoys her if it is "way too long,"
   uses "big words that I clearly didn't say," or "AI grammar like em dashes that I would never
   use."
2. **Math tutoring.** Intercepts of h(x) = 2^(x+2) − 3. "It's good when it's easy to understand and
   not a lot of text. I learn a lot better when it's a visual."

She rated by talking, not clicking:

| Test | Best | Second | Worst |
|---|---|---|---|

### What the run shows

- **The judges and she disagree on math.** They scored GPT-5's ASCII graph 9 to 10; she ranked it
  worst. For her, "visual" means clean layout, not ASCII art. This supports showing only her picks.
- **The judges ignored her em-dash rule** on the email test, even though it was in her criteria.
- **Claude Sonnet 4.5 was the fastest and cheapest on both tests and second on both.** Across both
  tests: Claude $0.011, GPT-5 $0.026, Gemini $0.026. With speed and cost as a tiebreaker (see Other
  signals), the tie would have broken toward Claude, which is also the single model she
  asked for.

## What they actually do today

- **Business:** "I tell Claude to go in and read my unread emails and write me drafts" and then she
  checks and sends them herself. Her drafts folder was full of Claude-written replies.
- **School:** gives Claude her reviews or homework; it makes interactive graphs and walks her
  through problems step by step.
- Uses Claude mainly because she has more usage there than on ChatGPT, but "ChatGPT gives it to me
  a lot more simply for me to understand." Even when she asks Claude for something super visual
  it is "a ton of text," and if she doesn't say otherwise it makes "a visual PDF, which doesn't
  help me at all."
- Dictates with Wispr Flow; she and I would both pay about $20 to $30 a month for it.

## Strongest signal

A tie is not an answer for her. "I would rather just have 1. ... tell me which would be the best
for both my tasks. One singular, one model." She will only switch models freely if they live in one
harness ("If it was the same harness, then that would be fine").

## Other signals

- **Talking beats checkboxes.** Asked whether checkboxes or checkboxes-plus-comments would be
  easier: "No, I like to say," "I think this is what I would prefer."
- **Her rubric is personal and the judges miss it.** The judges rewarded the ASCII graph and never
  penalized em dashes, the two things she cared most about.
- **She liked it.** "This is sick, honey. That's a really cool idea. I like it a lot." "It's like
  an I test."
- **Speed and cost should count** (my note, she agreed): when two answers are about equally good,
  like the first two emails, the result should point to the faster, cheaper one. Claude Sonnet 4.5
  was the fastest and cheapest on both tests.
- **The lineup was not frontier.** None of the three models is the newest from its vendor.
- She was curious about the "test on your own Claude plan" path (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus) as well.

    "visual means clear layout, not ASCII art").
