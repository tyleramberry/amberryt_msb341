## Transcript

**Tyler:** How are you?
**Student:** Doing well, thanks. What's on your mind?

**Tyler:** Let's get into a few questions. What was the last written assignment you had to submit?
**Student:** A 3-page memo for my investments class. We had to pick a stock and argue whether it's undervalued right now, with a DCF and comps to back it up. I chose Costco. I basically wrote the whole thing the night before it was due.

**Tyler:** What did that process look like, from finding out about the assignment to submitting it?
**Student:** The professor posted it on Learning Suite, which is what BYU uses. I saw it when I checked the course page that evening, skimmed it, and figured out the deadline. I started by pulling Costco's latest 10-K and their last few earnings calls to get the numbers. Then I built the DCF in Excel: I forecast revenue growth, margins and free cash flow, and discounted it back at my WACC. For comps I looked at Target, Walmart and Dollar General, mostly on EV/EBITDA and P/E. I wrote the memo in Google Docs over the weekend, had a friend read it once, and submitted it as a PDF through Learning Suite's assignment portal.

**Tyler:** When you know what the professor wants but aren't sure your draft meets those expectations, what do you do?
**Student:** I take it to office hours with specific spots marked, like "does this paragraph actually answer the prompt?" That way I'm not asking them to re-read the whole thing.

**Tyler:** When you've used AI on schoolwork, have you ever worried about how a professor would take it, and what did you do about it?
**Student:** Yeah, definitely. My big one was a stats problem set where I used ChatGPT to check my approach on a regression, and I kept thinking that if he saw the pattern in my work, he'd know. So I rewrote everything in my own words and redid the steps by hand so the logic was actually mine, even if the idea came from somewhere else. I also got more honest with myself about where the line is. Using it to explain a concept is one thing, but letting it produce the answer is another.

**Tyler:** Which of your courses this term are on Canvas, and how many are on Learning Suite?
**Student:** Four on Learning Suite and two on Canvas. The Canvas ones are my econ elective and a writing class. Everything else for the major runs through Learning Suite.

**Tyler:** Are you paying for any tools right now that help with school, like Grammarly, a tutor or an AI subscription?
**Student:** Just Grammarly Premium, about $15 a month. I tried a Chegg subscription sophomore year but canceled it, because it felt like I was paying to skip the thinking. I don't have an AI subscription right now, though I've thought about it.

**Tyler:** If you were going to pay for a product that would help you with school, what would it have to do to be worthwhile?
**Student:** It would have to save me time on the boring parts without touching the parts that count for my grade, like auto-formatting citations or pulling the right data from a 10-K into a spreadsheet. That's stuff that eats an hour but teaches me nothing. If it started doing my analysis or writing my arguments, that's where I'd stop trusting it.

**Tyler:** Are there any functions or features you'd like it to have?
**Student:** A built-in citation checker that actually works with BYU's preferred style would be huge. And maybe a way to paste in a rubric and have it flag where my draft is thin before I submit.

**Tyler:** Got it. Thanks for your help. That's all.
**Student:** Good luck with everything. Take care.

## Key takeaways

- **Workflow:** The assignment shows up on Learning Suite, the student pulls source data (10-K, earnings calls), does the analysis in Excel, writes in Google Docs, gets one review from a friend, and submits a PDF to Learning Suite.
- **Procrastination:** The memo was written mostly the night before it was due.
- **Checking a draft against the rubric:** The student goes to office hours with specific spots flagged and wants targeted feedback, not a full re-read.
- **AI anxiety:** The student worries that professors can spot AI help, so they rewrite and redo work by hand. Their line is "explain a concept" yes, "produce the answer" no.
- **LMS mix:** Four courses on Learning Suite and two on Canvas. Learning Suite carries the major's courses.
- **Current spend:** Grammarly Premium at about $15 a month. They canceled Chegg because it felt like "paying to skip the thinking," and have no AI subscription yet.
- **What's worth paying for:** Saving time on low-learning busywork (citations, data extraction) while staying away from graded thinking (analysis, arguments).
- **Requested features:** A citation checker that matches BYU's preferred style, and a rubric-vs-draft gap checker to run before submitting.
