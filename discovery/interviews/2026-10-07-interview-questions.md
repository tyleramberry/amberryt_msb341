# Homeroom — Pre-Product User Interview Questions

**Date:** 2026-10-07
**Status:** Script for the first discovery interview (Sprint 2, pre-product)
**Purpose:** Test the problem, not the idea. No feature talk, no pitching Homeroom.
**Source:** designed from `discovery/icp.md` + `discovery/jtbd.md` validate-lists, run against Mom Test rules (behavior and specifics, never hypotheticals).

## Ground rules

- Talk about their life, not Homeroom. Do not describe the product.
- Ask about the past, never "would you use…" / "would you pay for…".
- Talk less than 20% of the time. Dig with "tell me more", "give me an example".
- Record their words, not your interpretation.

## The five questions

### 1. Walk me through the last written assignment you had to turn in — what did the process look like from finding out about it to hitting submit?

Tests frequency and intensity of the rubric-graded paper loop.
Strong: a specific paper narrated unprompted; the late-night uncertainty is memorable.
Weak: can't recall one, or it took an hour with no friction.

### 2. What did you do when you weren't sure your draft hit the criteria — did you paste anything into ChatGPT or Gemini, or ask someone?

Tests the current workaround and the re-pasting pain (functional layer of the JTBD).
Get screenshot-level detail: where they paste, what they typed, what came back, whether it knew the course.

### 3. When you've used AI on schoolwork, have you ever worried about how a professor would take it? What did you do about that?

Tests the social / academic-integrity layer — the reason for the tutor-first persona.
Strong: it comes up unprompted.
Weak: they shrug, which would mean the integrity angle is weaker than the PRD assumes.

### 4. Which of your courses this term are on Canvas, and which are on Learning Suite? Have you ever tried to get an access token or connect anything to Canvas?

Tests the biggest listed risk (Canvas coverage + whether student tokens work). Disqualifier question, ask early.
If most courses are Learning Suite, the F1 dependency breaks and the ICP doesn't hold.

### 5. What are you paying for to help you with school right now — and what did the last thing you paid for actually get you?

Tests willingness to pay from real spend, not a hypothetical number.
Never ask "would you pay for this". Ask what they already pay.

## Close-out

- Ask permission to follow up in a week (that is the beta-tester list).
- "Who else do you know who has this problem?" — referrals satisfy the Access Test.
- Fill in an interview note from `000-interview-template.md` the same day, while their words are fresh.
