## What I asked / what happened

Five-question round against the pre-product script. They answered all five without much
probing, and the answers were concrete: one specific assignment, one specific workaround, one
specific spend figure ($0). See **Method caveats** for where the interview drifted from the
script.

## What they actually do today

Their last written assignment was a "good news" customer-service reply email in a business
writing course (reply to a customer with an order problem, using a "you" attitude, concise).
Found on Canvas, due in 1–2 days, written and submitted the same day.

The workflow:

1. Read the prompt, then read the supplied company policies and background in a split screen,
   going back and forth between the two while drafting.
2. Wrote a first draft themselves.
3. Pasted **the assignment prompt and their draft** into AI and asked for improvement
   suggestions: grammar, spelling, anything to change.
4. AI returned a draft close to their own; they copied the AI version, then edited it back
   toward their own voice because the AI version "was also like AI vibe."
5. Submitted on **Peerceptiv**, not Canvas.

For the rubric-check job, unprompted and stated plainly:

> "I'll copy the rubric and put it into Claude."
> — asked whether they do this for every assignment: "for most, yeah. Just, like, tell me
> what I'm missing. What I need."

Integrity pressure, in their words:

> "sometimes the guidelines on AI are kind of blurred. Like, okay, it's allowed for the
> class, but like how much — so sometimes I worry about that."
> "I never just turn in, like, full copy-paste AI things."
> On last-minute AI help: "I don't have a ton of time to revise it, and I worry that I'm
> missing, like, giveaways of AI."

Canvas access: has never tried to get an API token or connect anything; didn't know it was
possible. (Q4 came back thin — see caveats.)

Spend on school tools today: **$0.** Grammarly free version, possibly not even installed
anymore; ChatGPT free; Wispr Flow free; uses someone else's paid Claude Pro.

## Strongest signal

**They already do the core job by hand, weekly, for free.** Pasting the rubric into a chatbot
and asking "tell me what I'm missing" is exactly the F1 loop Homeroom automates — the first
behavioral confirmation of the JTBD, and it came without me asking about rubrics.

Counterweight in the same interview: they pay nothing, and the thing they say they'd need
before paying is **memory** ("knows me"), plus a visual interface — they explicitly say
ChatGPT teaches them better than Claude and can't say why. This interview produced no real
spend to anchor price on.

## Method caveats (script deviations — record as bets, not evidence)

1. **Opened with a product description.** The script's ground rules say talk less than 20% and
   never describe the product. The intro described the product and the "AI at 30 minutes to
   deadline, no learning happens" thesis, which frames every answer after it.
2. **Closed with a hypothetical feature question.** "Would it be helpful if it had
   pre-knowledge of all your rubrics, of all your due dates?" is the leading shape the course's
   chapter names as a bad question; the answer ("a good memory, yeah… It would need that for me
   to pay for it") is polite agreement and should be logged as a bet.
3. **Q4 came back thin.** Canvas-vs-Learning-Suite coverage was never established — the
   question slid into "have you tried to access the Canvas API token," which is a different
   question. The disqualifier check effectively didn't happen. Needed on the next interview.
4. **Not an outside ICP interview.** Household convenience sample, using the interviewer's paid
   Claude account. Pilot of the script, not market evidence.
